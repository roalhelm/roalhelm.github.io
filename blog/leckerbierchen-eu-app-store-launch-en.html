LeckerBierchen is now on the App Store in the EU

It is done: the Digital Services Act approval has gone through, and LeckerBierchen is finally available on the App Store in Germany and the EU.

In my last post I explained why technical approval is not the same as release in Germany. Apple had already approved the app technically, but DSA compliance was still missing for distribution in the EU. That step is now complete. “Technically ready” has finally become “available”.

What LeckerBierchen does

As a reminder: LeckerBierchen is a simple iPhone and iPad app for bar staff and waiters. It is meant to help during service without getting in the way.

  • Create tables and guests
  • Record orders and keep open amounts in view, updated live
  • Use a quick checkout for walk-in customers
  • Settle open tabs clearly
  • Maintain the drinks menu, including a photo scan that runs locally on the device

The app runs locally on the device. There is no account, no cloud, and no external AI service for menu recognition. Privacy, stability, and fast operation were the focus from day one.

What I take away from the journey

Getting into the App Store took much longer than I expected at the start. Code and features were only part of it. There was also App Store Connect, metadata, privacy details, a support page, an imprint, the Apple review, and finally the Digital Services Act.

That was the real lesson for me: a release is not only a technical event, it is also a matter of product, compliance, and operations. And it is worth walking that path completely yourself at least once.

The first feature requests are in

The best part of the release: other people are now using the app, and the first feature requests have already arrived. That is exactly why you do this. Feedback from real use shows what is actually missing in day-to-day service much faster than any test at a desk.

I am currently collecting and evaluating these requests and planning them into the next versions. The goal stays the same: LeckerBierchen should become even more practical in service without losing the simplicity that defines it. Not every idea will be built right away, but every one will be looked at.

If you use the app and have an idea, or something does not work as expected, I would love to hear from you. You will find all contact options on the support page.

What comes next

Next up are planning the feature requests and shipping the first updates. I will keep writing about the progress here on the blog. Cheers to the release! 🍻
