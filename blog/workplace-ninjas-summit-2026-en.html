What am I taking away from Workplace Ninja Summit 2026?

Four intense days at Workplace Ninja Summit 2026 are over.

One topic was almost everywhere over the last few days: AI.

From AI agents to tool generation and vibecoding, it became clear that our jobs and ways of working will change. The question is no longer whether that will happen, but how we shape that change in a useful, secure, and high-quality way.

What I found especially interesting were the discussions around security, governance, and responsibility. The easier it becomes to create tools, automations, and code, the more important standards, traceability, and security become.

AI agents are no longer a gimmick

One takeaway was reinforced for me during the event:

AI agents are no longer a gimmick.

They are evolving from assistants into active participants in processes. They create content, generate tools, make pre-decisions, and orchestrate different systems.

But that is also where the challenge lies.

The more responsibility we hand over to AI, the more important control, monitoring, and clear boundaries become. A single agent mistake can now have effects far beyond one broken script.

Vibecoding is impressive and dangerous at the same time

I see vibecoding in a similar way.

The speed at which applications, scripts, and automations can be created today is impressive.

At the same time, that is exactly what makes me pause.

The barrier to building something is dropping rapidly.

But the responsibility for quality, maintainability, and security does not disappear.

Quite the opposite.

When production code can be created in a matter of minutes, we need to ask questions like:

  • Is the code understandable?
  • Is it secure?
  • Would I run this in production?
  • Do I still fully understand what is happening?

This was not a new realization for me.

But many talks and discussions reinforced what I had already suspected and at the same time made me think again.

The real work starts now

Like many others, I still have some homework to do.

The last few days showed me that I am generally on the right path. At the same time, there is still enough room to critically review my own tools, automations, and projects.

That is why I will be revisiting many of my repositories over the coming weeks.

Not because they do not work.

But because they may no longer provide the value I originally planned.

Some projects will be expanded.

Some modernized.

And some may even be retired on purpose.

Because one takeaway is definitely clear to me:

Quality over quantity.

A well-maintained repository that creates real value is far more valuable in the long run than ten projects that nobody maintains or understands anymore.

The most important part remains the community

As exciting as the technical topics were, my personal highlight remains the community.

The conversations with former colleagues, long-time companions, and new contacts were at least as valuable as many of the sessions.

Many ideas are not born on stage.

They emerge over coffee, at dinner, or in discussions with people facing similar challenges.

That is exactly why community events like Workplace Ninja Summit are so important to me.

They do not just deliver knowledge.

They help you ask the right questions.

And sometimes that is more valuable than any answer.
